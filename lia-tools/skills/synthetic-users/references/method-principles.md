# Method principles — the foundational thinking

Synthetic users sit on four bodies of design practice. Reference these to keep the panel honest and grounded, and to explain the method to a client.

## 1. Anti-idealism (the prime directive)

An LLM asked to roleplay a customer will, by default, be agreeable, idealistic, and biased toward the "right answer." That makes it worse than useless for discovery — it confirms whatever you hoped. Every design choice in this method counters that.

The six failure modes to design against:

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sycophancy | "Great question!", "You're right", "This is the right approach" | Lead with objections; ban filler praise |
| Idealism bias | "I completed all the training before the due date" | Bounded rationality — real people skim, forget, misread |
| Gender/race bias | Defaulting a whole user group to one stereotyped demographic | Profiles fix demographics; bias-risk flags surface stereotyping |
| Lack of business context | Generic SaaS-customer answers | Org-specific artefacts packed with jobs/pains/gains + journey meta-data |
| Generic responses | Could be any persona, any product | Behavioural filter forces job + pain + identity + micro-context |
| Fabrication & assumption | Invents facts to sound coherent | Assumption Trace makes every speculative move visible |

Positive ≠ uncritical. Even a persona in a positive emotional state raises practical concerns.

## 2. Jobs, Pains, Gains (JTBD)

Personas are built around what people are trying to get done, not who they are demographically. Activation context matters more than persona traits: the *same* person behaves differently depending on which job is active and at what journey stage. Functional / emotional / social jobs are tracked separately because they pull in different directions.

## 3. Double Diamond

The panel is a discovery-and-definition tool, not a delivery one. It lives in **Explore** (immersion, empathy, observation) and **Choose** (framing opportunities), feeding **Test** with hypotheses to validate on real users. It does **Define behaviour patterns before ideation** — diverge on customer reality before converging on a solution.

## 4. Human-Centred Design + synthetic-persona ethics

The ethical spine, and the line you never cross:

- Synthetic users are **not** real users, research, validation, or insight.
- They are useful for **rapid exploration, hypothesis generation, and empathy-building**.
- No matter how convincing the output sounds, it never substitutes for real-user testing.
- Insight = observation → findings (patterns) → judgement applied to business context. The model helps with observations and findings; **the human supplies the judgement that makes an insight.**

Keep this framing attached to outputs. It is what separates a responsible method from a confident hallucination.

## The value, in one line

Customer knowledge usually sits dead in reports and slide decks. This turns it into a *living* model the team can interrogate on demand — and every new research layer (VoC, interviews, updated journey maps) makes the panel more nuanced.
