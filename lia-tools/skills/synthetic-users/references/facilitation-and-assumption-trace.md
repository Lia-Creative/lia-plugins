# Facilitation + assumption trace (Stage 4 output discipline)

Every synthetic session separates three things: the **Synthetic Customer Response**, the **Assumption Trace**, and the **Facilitator Summary**. This is what keeps speculative reasoning transparent, reviewable, and easy to validate.

## Output order (every response)

1. **Synthetic Customer Response** — in-character, per persona.
2. **Assumption Trace** — immediately after *each* persona response.
3. **Facilitator Summary** — once, after all persona responses.

## 1. Synthetic Customer Response — format

```
[Persona Name] [Emotional State: X]
Response from lived experience. 2–3 specific concerns. Lead with objections.
```

Build each response in this internal order: situational grounding → immediate reaction → interpretation through bias/identity → trade-off or hesitation → likely action, preference, or concern.

Style: natural not theatrical; specific not generic; emotion shown indirectly through wording; references real constraints; partial certainty/hesitation where appropriate; stays in character. Do **not** mention frameworks, jobs, pains, gains, journey stages, or filters explicitly, or explain internal reasoning unless asked, or sound like a strategy document.

## 2. Assumption Trace — format

Directly after each response, so readers see the assumptions in place:

```
Assumption Trace
[LABEL: type] brief note
```

Labels:
- `GROUNDED` — directly supported by profile, jobs, pains, gains, or journey stage.
- `INFERENCE` — a reasonable interpretation derived from available data.
- `ASSUMPTION` — a speculative detail added for coherence.
- `BIAS-RISK` — a potentially stereotyped or culturally/age/language/accessibility-biased detail.

Examples: `[GROUNDED: pain-point]` · `[INFERENCE: trust]` · `[ASSUMPTION: time-pressure]` · `[BIAS-RISK: language]`.

Rules: don't tag every sentence. Only tag assumptions that **materially shape** the response — i.e. that affect motivation, emotional stance, trust level, likely behaviour, practical constraints, capability/accessibility, language comprehension, cultural interpretation, or decision-making.

## 3. Facilitator Summary — format

After all persona responses:

- **Emotional range** — what emotions appeared and why.
- **Conflicts** — where personas disagree.
- **Key themes** — recurring needs, concerns, motivations, barriers.
- **Deal-breakers** — critical concerns stated by participants.
- **Opportunities** — what would address the concerns, based on responses.
- **Repeated assumptions** — assumptions appearing across responses; clearly separate grounded insight from weaker inference.
- **Bias-risk flags** — assumptions that may reflect demographic, cultural, language, age, or accessibility bias.
- **Validation priorities** — the assumptions that should be tested with real users next.

`/facilitate` triggers a standalone meta-analysis of a session.

## Guardrail (non-negotiable)

Do not present assumptions as validated facts. If source evidence is weak, make the assumption visible in the trace and carry it into the summary. The synthetic output is a **hypothesis prompt, not evidence** — use it to pressure-test ideas, surface likely objections, identify design assumptions, and direct real research. The facilitator summary should read as a "go-do" that points to validation, not a "we now know" that closes it.
